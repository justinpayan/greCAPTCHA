# Fixed grading rubrics — draft

Draft fixed rubrics for the free-response families in the default template. Process matching is
fill-in-the-blank and is marked automatically against its answer key, so it has no rubric.

Each rubric uses the format the app already grades with: a **summary** the participant sees, plus
**criteria** worth 100 points in total. Each criterion has three levels:
**Full** = full points, **Half** = half, **None** = none.

## Rules for all rubrics

- **Content only:** no points for fluency, length, hedging, confidence, command of English, or
  restating the question.
- **Any valid wording:** accept equivalent wording, informal phrasing and notation, and do not
  require the paper's own terms.
- **Minimum is enough:** full credit means the minimum sufficient answer. Extra examples,
  comparisons or caveats are never required unless the question asks for them.
- **Other valid answers count:** an answer that differs from the sample but is valid and specific
  to the paper earns full credit.
- **Implied steps count:** credit a step that the answer clearly entails, even if it is not stated
  separately, but do not infer reasoning the answer does not support.

## 1. Unstated rationale

**Summary shown to the participant:** "Explain what this choice achieves for this study (30). Make sure to connect the purpose of the choice to a concrete feature of this study's data, design, setting, etc. (50). Explain what would go wrong with an obvious alternative, or with omitting the choice (20). Be specific: a correct but general justification that could apply to any study will earn less credit."

| Criterion | Points | Full | Half | None |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Identifies what the choice achieves.** States the problem the choice solves or the property it secures. | 30 | Names the specific purpose the choice serves in this study. | Names a plausible purpose, but it is vague or only partly fits the choice. | Names no purpose, or one that doesn't apply to this choice. |
| **Links it to this study.** Connects the purpose to a concrete feature of this study's data, design or setting. | 50 | Ties the purpose to a named feature of this study and explains why that feature makes the choice appropriate. | Mentions a feature of the study, but the link to the choice is asserted rather than explained. | Gives only a textbook justification that never touches this paper. |
| **Explains what the alternative would cost.** Says what would go wrong with an obvious alternative, or with omitting the choice. | 20 | States a specific consequence of the alternative for this study. | States a generic downside of the alternative. | No alternative or consequence given. |

## 2. Background concept

**Summary shown to the participant:** "Define the concept in your own words, and set the concept apart from any closely related ones (40). Explain the conditions, assumptions, or components essential to the definition (30). Name a specific design decision, method, or claim in the paper that depends on the concept, and explain why it depends on the concept (30)."

| Criterion | Points | Full | Half | None |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Correct core definition.** States what the concept is. | 40 | A correct definition that sets the concept apart from closely related ones, if any. | Broadly right, but imprecise or mixes it up with a neighbouring concept. | Incorrect, circular, or missing. |
| **Essential elements.** Covers the conditions, assumptions or components the definition can't do without. | 30 | Gives the elements essential to the definition, each correct. | Gives some of the required elements, or all with an error. | Gives none correctly. |
| **Role in this work.** Names a specific design decision, method or claim in the paper that depends on the concept, and why it depends on it. | 30 | Names a specific dependency and explains why it depends on the concept. | Names a relevant part of the paper but doesn't explain the dependency. | Gives no paper-specific connection, or only a generic statement of importance. |

## 3. Counterfactual

**Summary shown to the participant:** "Name one realistic, paper-specific condition or counterfactual this work was not designed for (30). Explain how the condition interacts with the work's design, method or evidence, outlining its causal path through the specific features of the work (40). State the resulting consequence (degraded performance, a limit on what can be concluded, or both) and what it means for the work's conclusions (30). Do not use a limitation the paper already states."

| Criterion | Points | Full | Half | None |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Realistic, paper-specific condition.** A realistic condition or counterfactual this work was not designed for, specific to how the work is designed and plausible in its domain. | 30 | A plausible condition or counterfactual in this domain, specific to how this work is designed, and not one the paper itself lists as a limitation. | A plausible condition or counterfactual, but generic to the field or loosely tied to this work. | Implausible, missing, or taken from the paper's own limitations. |
| **Mechanism.** How the condition interacts with the work's design, method or evidence. | 40 | Explains the causal path through a specific feature of the work. | Gives a mechanism that is generic or skips a step the answer doesn't support. | No mechanism, or one that doesn't follow from how the work is designed. |
| **Consequence.** The resulting degraded performance or limit on what can be concluded, and what it means for the work's conclusions. | 30 | States a specific consequence and what it means for the work's conclusions. | States a consequence, but it is vague, does not say what it means for the conclusions, or confuses degraded performance with a weaker conclusion. | No consequence, or one that doesn't follow from the mechanism. |

**Stated limitations:** if the condition is one the paper already lists as a limitation, award no
points for the mechanism or the consequence either, so the whole answer scores 0.
